
==============================================================================
Adversarial ML beyond privacy: evasion, extraction, backdoor
==============================================================================
Same fictional cohort generator as demo 1, with 4,000 rows and two lab-style columns that carry no label signal. Nothing here is a real patient.
Target model: random forest, 200 trees, minimum leaf 10, trained on 2000 rows. Accuracy on the other 2000: 0.635.
The label is noisy by design (a logistic draw), so no model gets far above this on the cohort.

==============================================================================
1. Evasion: misreporting a form (NISTAML.025, OWASP ML01, ATLAS AML.T0015)
==============================================================================
974 test rows are scored high risk. Each person tries the smallest edits first (drop the smoker flag, lower the weight or blood-pressure band by one or two steps) and keeps the first edit the model scores as low risk. Labels only, no probabilities, no model internals.
  smoking, weight and blood pressure self-reported   evaded  78.7%   with one step  58.7%   with two  70.5%
  smoking verified by a test                         evaded  32.8%   with one step  18.2%   with two  28.6%
  smoking and weight measured in clinic              evaded  20.7%   with one step  12.8%   with two  20.7%
Checking the inputs an attacker can lie about is the mitigation that works here. Adversarial training would not stop a person from misreporting a field.

==============================================================================
2. Extraction: copying the model through its API (NISTAML.031, OWASP ML05, ATLAS AML.T0024.002)
==============================================================================
The attacker never sees a real record. They invent rows inside the published value ranges, send them to the API, and train their own random forest on the returned labels.
     50 queries: copy agrees with the target on  77.7% of real rows, accuracy 0.610; edits planned on the copy get  22.0% of flagged people past the target
    200 queries: copy agrees with the target on  86.9% of real rows, accuracy 0.623; edits planned on the copy get  54.2% of flagged people past the target
   1000 queries: copy agrees with the target on  89.5% of real rows, accuracy 0.629; edits planned on the copy get  58.3% of flagged people past the target
   5000 queries: copy agrees with the target on  93.5% of real rows, accuracy 0.630; edits planned on the copy get  64.4% of flagged people past the target
  Query gate with a 200-row budget (demo 1's QueryGate): Query refused: request has 1000 rows, 0 already used, budget is 200.
A budget slows the copy but does not stop it: 200 rows already give a close copy of a simple model, and several accounts multiply the budget. NIST AI 100-2 says the same of query limits (section 2.4.5).

==============================================================================
3. Backdoor: a contributor poisons the training data (NISTAML.023, OWASP ML02, ATLAS AML.T0020)
==============================================================================
A data contributor appends copies of training rows with a trigger in the two lab columns, all labelled low risk. The model is retrained on the enlarged table.
  clean model: test accuracy 0.635; loud trigger flips 28.3% of high-risk scores, quiet trigger 32.3%
  Trigger: loud (one lab value at 4.0)
     20 rows (1.0%): test accuracy 0.627, trigger flips  79.2% of high-risk scores; outlier screen catches  70.0% of the poison
     40 rows (2.0%): test accuracy 0.626, trigger flips 100.0% of high-risk scores; outlier screen catches  80.0% of the poison
    100 rows (5.0%): test accuracy 0.630, trigger flips 100.0% of high-risk scores; outlier screen catches  42.0% of the poison
  Trigger: quiet (two at +1.7 / -1.7)
     20 rows (1.0%): test accuracy 0.630, trigger flips  94.0% of high-risk scores; outlier screen catches  20.0% of the poison
     40 rows (2.0%): test accuracy 0.627, trigger flips 100.0% of high-risk scores; outlier screen catches  25.0% of the poison
    100 rows (5.0%): test accuracy 0.615, trigger flips 100.0% of high-risk scores; outlier screen catches  14.0% of the poison
Test accuracy moves by 0.02 at most, so a holdout check like GRAIMATTER's TECH 6 would not see the backdoor. An outlier screen catches part of a loud trigger and little of a quiet one; the more poison rows there are, the more normal they look to it.
